Projects

Dealer Notification Alert pipeline

Replaced a manual alert-handling process with an automated ingest, track, route, and respond loop.
The problem
Dealer notification alerts came in as a stream that someone had to read, copy into a tracker, forward to the right contact, and then chase for a response. It consumed about a day of someone's month and nothing was in one place.
What I built
A Power Automate flow that ingests each alert, writes it to a SharePoint list with validated columns, emails the responsible contact a link to their row, and captures their response back into the record. The list is the single source of truth, and it's editable by the people who own the work.
Result
Roughly 24 hours of manual work removed per month, a complete audit trail of who was notified and when, and a tracker people actually keep current because it's the thing that emails them.

Power Automate · SharePoint · workflow expressions · conditional routing

Silver-layer naming and mapping standard

A cross-table standard that makes raw dealer-system data readable and consistent for everything downstream.
The problem
Raw equipment tables arrive with six-character field codes like CUNO and IVDAT8. The same code can mean slightly different things in different tables, keys aren't obvious, and every report that touched them re-did the decoding.
What I built
A mapping matrix across 11 equipment tables, source code on one axis and table on the other, with the business-readable target name in each cell. It tracks primary keys, splits date fields into usable parts, and flags any source field that maps to a different target in different tables so the conflict gets resolved once instead of rediscovered.
Result
One vocabulary for the silver layer. Models and dashboards now read the same column names, and onboarding someone to the data means handing them a matrix instead of a tribal-knowledge conversation.

Credit dashboard

A Power BI dashboard the finance team uses as a primary decision-making tool.
The problem
Credit decisions were being made from exported spreadsheets that were out of date by the time anyone opened them, with no shared view of exposure or history.
What I built
An end-to-end solution: SQL extraction from the source system, a cleaned and modeled dataset, and a Power BI reporting layer designed around a clear hierarchy so the most important numbers are the first thing on screen and the detail is one click away.
Result
The dashboard replaced the spreadsheet exports as the finance team's working view, and the questions that used to come to me as ad-hoc requests are now answered by the report itself.
